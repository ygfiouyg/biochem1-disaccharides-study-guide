```
Disaccharides
A disaccharide is the carbohydrate formed when two
monosaccharide undergo a condensation reaction
which involves the elimination of water.

Three common examples are sucrose, lactose and
maltose.
```


## Page 04

```
There are two different types
of disaccharides:
• Reducing disaccharides: In which one monosaccharide,

 has a free hydroxyl group at the anomeric carbon.

 Examples include lactose and maltose.

• Non-Reducing disaccharides: There is no free hydroxyl

 group at the anomeric carbon in the molecule
```

```
Maltose ( malt sugar )
• Maltose is found in germinating seeds such as barley.

• Malose is a disaccharide formed from two units of

• glucose joined with an α(1→4) bond, formed from a
 condensation reaction.
```

## Page 08

```
Lactose
• Lactose is a disaccharide sugar that is found most notably
  in milk.

• Lactose is extracted from sweet or sour whey.

• The Latin word for milk, plus the -ose ending used to
  name sugars.
```


## Page 09

```
Lactose is derived from the condensation of galactose
and glucose, which form a β-1→4 glycosidic linkage.

Its systematic name is β-D-galactopyranosyl-(1→4)-D-
glucose
```

## Page 14

```
Sucrose (Non-Reducing
disaccharide):
• Sucrose (Cane sugar):

• It is formed by the condensation of a molecule of α-D-
 glucopyranose with a molecule of β-D-fructofuranose
 through α-β1-2-glucosidic linkage.
```


## Page 15

```
• Sucrose is dextrorotatory (d) , and on hydrolysis gives a
 levorotatory (L) mixture of equal amount of glucose and
 fructose.

• This mixture is called invert sugar, and the enzyme
 producing hydrolysis is called invertase, due to the
 inversion of rotation form dextro to levo during
 hydrolysis.
```

```
3. Ketose test : Procedure :

Add 2 ml of sugar solution to 1 ml of conc HCl solution. Boil
for 2 min.

Observation: reddish brown.

Comment: HCl hydrolyses sucrose to glucose and fructose.

• Dehydration of fructose and formation of furfural which
 is reddish brown in color in acidic medium.
```


## Page 19

```
4.  Acid hydrolysis: Procedure:
1.  Add 5 ml of sucrose, in a test tube
2.  add 2 ml of dilute sulfuric acid.
3.  Boil for 2 minutes then Cool thoroughly under the tap.
4.  Neutralize by adding 2 ml of 30% NaOH.
5.  Take about 2 ml of the hydrolysate and add 2ml of the mixed
    Fehling's reagent and boil.
Observation: Red ppt
• N.B:It is essential to neutralize the hydrolysate with NaOH
  before applying Fehling's solution as excess acid will dissolve
  cuprous oxide.
• Comment:
• The acid hydrolyses sucrose to glucose and fructose & both of
  them reduce Cu+2 to Cu2O (red ppt).
```
